Good and Bad Examples

Concrete examples for common fields, comments, and content choices.

Page title and slug

Good

Title: Data Governance FAQ
Slug: data-governance-faq

Bad

Title: FAQ
Slug: page1

Why: The good example is descriptive and stable. The bad example is ambiguous. This is recommended naming guidance.

Approval comment

Good

Reviewed version 3 for required legal wording and external links. No changes are required for the reviewed scope.

Bad

Looks good.

Why: The good comment identifies version, scope, and result and is long enough. The bad comment does not explain the review.

Revision request

Good

Update the retention statement in block 6 to match the approved policy, correct the owner name, and resubmit a new version.

Bad

Fix this.

Why: The good comment is actionable. The bad comment offers no location or required correction.

Rejection comment

Good

Rejected because the version describes the wrong policy owner and omits the required disclaimer. Create a corrected version using the confirmed owner and approved disclaimer.

Bad

Rejected.

Why: The good example explains why the version cannot proceed and what must change.

Good

Diagram showing Draft, Content Review, Approved, and Published stages connected in sequence.

Bad

Why: The good example communicates the image meaning. The bad example repeats a filename.

Version summary

Good

Updates security-review language and replaces the expired canonical link.

Bad

Changes

Why: The good example helps reviewers understand the purpose of the version.
